# Follow-Up Decisions: Three Cases

Source: [cases.md](cases.md), fictional.

## Case A: Dev Malhotra

**Decision: Follow up now.** Two weeks of plain silence with no stated reason either way, and no question of theirs left unanswered. There is a genuine reason to reach out.

**Weak anchor (do not send this):** "Hi Dev, just following up on your volunteer sign-up, let me know if you're still interested!"

**Strong anchor:** "Hi Dev, thanks again for signing up to help with Saturday sorting, we've got a shift starting at 9am this weekend if you're still able to join, no pressure if your plans have changed."

The strong version names the actual thing (the Saturday shift, a real time), rather than a generic nudge about the sign-up itself.

## Case B: Alicia Ferreira

**Decision: Answer something first.** Alicia asked a direct, specific question that was never answered. Sending a generic follow-up now, without answering it, would read as if her question was ignored, which it was. The answer comes before anything else.

**Message:** "Hi Alicia, sorry for the slow reply, yes, there's parking on site, and the number 12 bus also stops right outside if that's easier. Let us know if Saturday still works for you."

## Case C: Owen Bishop

**Decision: Stop.** Owen gave a clear, specific reason for withdrawing. There is no ambiguity here and no unanswered question, this is a stated decline, not silence.

**No follow-up message is drafted for this case.** Sending one anyway, even a polite one asking him to reconsider, would be ignoring what he actually said.

## Summary

Three different kinds of quiet needed three different responses: genuine silence with nothing left hanging (follow up, properly anchored to the actual shift), silence with a specific question still owed an answer (answer it first, do not nudge around it), and an explicit decline (stop, do not send anything).
